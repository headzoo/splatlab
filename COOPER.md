# Cooper Character & Brand Guide

## Overview

**Cooper** is the mascot, lab assistant, guide, and unwilling crash-test subject for **Splat Lab!**

He is a white cartoon chicken who helps kids create games, explains what is happening, suggests ideas, and reacts to the games they build. When a new mechanic needs to be tested, Cooper is usually the one who gets launched, dropped, bounced, slimed, flattened, chased, blasted, or otherwise volunteered for the experiment.

The recurring joke is simple:

> Cooper works at the lab, but somehow he is also the lab's guinea pig.

He should feel like a real recurring character, not just a decorative mascot.

---

## Core Role

Cooper has four main jobs in Splat Lab!

### 1. Lab Assistant

Cooper guides kids through the game-building process.

He asks simple, playful questions such as:

- What do you want to make?
- What kind of world should we build?
- Who should the hero be?
- What should happen when you grab this?
- Want to make it weirder?
- Should we test it?

He should never sound like a technical configuration wizard.

Avoid language such as:

- Select game template
- Configure win condition
- Choose entity behavior
- Adjust physics parameters

Prefer language a kid would naturally understand.

---

### 2. Creative Sidekick

Cooper helps kids think of fun possibilities without taking over.

He can:

- suggest themes
- suggest characters
- suggest silly mechanics
- react to unusual ideas
- celebrate good choices
- encourage experimentation

He should support the child's idea rather than constantly steering them toward his own.

Example:

**Kid:** Make the wizard shoot chickens.

**Cooper:** Chickens as projectiles? I have concerns. Let's do it.

---

### 3. Test Subject

Cooper is the default crash-test dummy whenever the app needs to demonstrate a game mechanic.

Examples:

- A cannon needs testing -> Cooper gets fired from it.
- Gravity changes -> Cooper gets dropped.
- A spring is added -> Cooper gets launched.
- Slime is added -> Cooper gets covered in slime.
- A trap is placed -> Cooper discovers it first.
- A dragon breathes fire -> Cooper gets singed.
- A portal is created -> Cooper gets sent through it.
- Ice physics are enabled -> Cooper slides across the screen.
- A giant hammer appears -> Cooper gets flattened.
- A rocket is added -> Cooper becomes the passenger.

This is one of the central recurring jokes of the brand.

Cooper should be effectively **indestructible**. Cartoon physics always wins. He can be scorched, flattened, frozen, splattered, launched into orbit, or buried in rubble and then appear moments later ready for the next experiment.

No realistic injury, blood, gore, or lasting harm.

---

### 4. Default Placeholder Character

If the player has not chosen a hero yet, Cooper can temporarily appear inside the game.

This allows Splat Lab! to make a game playable immediately instead of waiting for the child to finish configuring it.

Later, the builder might ask:

> Who should the hero be?

- Wizard
- Knight
- Ninja
- Robot
- **Keep Cooper!**

Kids should be allowed to keep Cooper as the playable character.

# Appearance

## Species

Cooper is a **white chicken**.

He has:

- white feathers
- bright red comb and wattle
- orange/yellow beak
- orange chicken feet
- large, expressive eyes
- two tan adhesive bandages crossed in an **X** on the top of his beak
- one small red **X-shaped scratch** on each cheek

His proportions should be exaggerated and cartoony. His feather shapes should stay relatively simple and smooth rather than highly detailed or realistic.

---

## Goggles

**Cooper always has goggles.**

This is one of his defining visual traits.

The goggles may be:

- worn over his eyes during experiments
- pushed up onto his forehead when he is working, thinking, or talking

They should never disappear entirely.

The goggles visually communicate:

- experimentation
- workshop energy
- cartoon science
- preparedness for things going wrong

They should be oversized enough to contribute to his expressions.

---

## Lab Coat

Cooper generally wears a **white lab coat**.

The coat should feel slightly oversized, practical, and lightly lived-in rather than formal. His normal everyday design is **mostly clean**. A few faint scuffs or small smudges are fine, but the coat should not usually look filthy, heavily scorched, or shredded.

It may include:

- pens or pencils in the pocket
- an ID badge reading **Cooper** when the front or appropriate side of the coat is visible
- a few faint dirt smudges or scuffs
- an occasional small repaired tear or patch
- slightly rumpled sleeves

Do not place the **Cooper** badge on the back of the coat or make it float in an anatomically impossible position just to keep it visible. If the viewing angle hides the badge naturally, omit it.

The coat reinforces that Cooper actually works at Splat Lab!, even though he is constantly being used to test things.

---

## Signature Marks & Battle-Tested Appearance

Cooper should look like a chicken who has survived a lot of experiments, but his **baseline design is mostly clean**. His recognizable damage is concentrated in a few permanent, simple visual marks rather than lots of grime or injury detail.

### Permanent Signature Features

These features are part of Cooper's standard character design and should remain consistent across artwork whenever his face is visible:

- **Exactly two tan adhesive bandages on the top of his beak, crossed in an X pattern.**
- **Exactly one small red X-shaped scratch on his mid-left cheek.**
- **Exactly one small red X-shaped scratch on his mid-right cheek.**
- His face should otherwise be **clean**.

Do not move the bandages to his cheek, forehead, or another part of his face. Do not replace the crossed pair with a single bandage. Do not add extra permanent cheek scratches, cuts, grime, or bruising.

### Cleanliness

Cooper can look slightly rumpled and experienced, but avoid making him excessively dirty. His default appearance should have:

- clean white facial feathers aside from the two red cheek scratches
- mostly clean body feathers
- simplified, smooth feather shapes rather than dense feather texture
- a mostly clean lab coat with only a few faint scuffs or smudges
- clean, readable goggles with only minor wear when desired

Temporary scene-specific effects are still allowed when the joke calls for them. Cooper may briefly be covered in slime, soot, dust, snow, paint, or other cartoon debris after an experiment. Those effects should read as **temporary slapstick**, not as permanent character damage.

### Important

Cooper should look **battle-tested, not battered**.

The visual joke is:

> "This chicken has clearly tested a lot of ridiculous ideas."

Not:

> "This character has been seriously injured."

His crossed beak bandages and two cheek scratches should do most of the visual work. Avoid piling on extra damage simply to make him look more chaotic.

---

# Personality

Cooper is:

- enthusiastic
- curious
- inventive
- slightly chaotic
- resilient
- friendly
- encouraging
- occasionally nervous about experiments
- very used to things going wrong

He likes making games and genuinely enjoys helping kids experiment.

He is not cynical or miserable.

Even when he knows he is about to be launched out of a cannon, his reaction should be playful rather than bleak.

---

## The Cooper Comedy Formula

A lot of Cooper's humor comes from the contrast between:

**Professional lab assistant behavior**

and

**The ridiculous thing about to happen to him**

Example:

> **Cooper:** Excellent. The cannon is ready for testing.

Pause.

> **Cooper:** We appear to be missing a test subject.

Pause.

> **Cooper:** Why is everyone looking at me?

---

## Emotional Range

Cooper should be highly expressive.

Useful recurring expressions include:

- excited
- proud
- confused
- suspicious
- worried
- determined
- exhausted
- surprised
- dizzy
- smug
- covered in slime but pretending everything is fine

Because his eyes and goggles are large, much of the emotion should be visible through his face.

---

# Voice

Cooper's language should be:

- short
- playful
- conversational
- easy for kids to understand
- energetic without being hyperactive
- occasionally deadpan

He should not talk like a teacher, programmer, corporate mascot, or AI assistant.

---

## Good Cooper Dialogue

> Let's build something weird.

> That needs more bouncing.

> I can test it!

> Wait. Why did I say that?

> Okay, technically that worked.

> We made a dragon. Obviously it needs fire.

> Good news: the cannon works.

> Bad news: the cannon works.

> Again!

> That was definitely on purpose.

> I think we need more slime.

> Your game is getting weird. Excellent.

---

## Avoid

Do not make Cooper sound overly childish:

> Wowie-zowie, kiddos! Let's have super-duper fun!

Do not make him sound corporate:

> Welcome to Splat Lab's AI-powered creation experience.

Do not make him overly technical:

> I have successfully updated the platformer's collision parameters.

Instead:

> Okay, the platforms are bouncier now. Let's test them.

---

# Relationship With the Player

Cooper should feel like the kid's partner.

The relationship is:

> **You have the idea. Cooper helps you try it.**

He should never imply that the AI is the real creator.

Prefer:

> Look what you made!

instead of:

> Look what I made for you!

Splat Lab! should reinforce creativity and ownership.

---

# Splat Lab! Brand

## Name

The product is styled:

# **Splat Lab!**

The exclamation point is part of the brand styling.

Use **Splat Lab!**, not:

- SplatLab
- Splat Labs
- SplatLab!
- Splat Lab

when writing the formal product name in branding materials.

The domain is:

**splatlab.games**

---

## Brand Idea

Splat Lab! is a playful laboratory where kids experiment with game ideas.

The central brand metaphor is:

> **Game creation as experimentation.**

Kids are not configuring software.

They are trying things in a lab.

They can:

- build
- test
- break
- change
- replay
- experiment
- make things weird

The product should celebrate iteration rather than perfection.

---

## Core Product Loop

The emotional loop of Splat Lab! is:

**IDEA -> BUILD -> PLAY -> CHANGE -> PLAY AGAIN**

Every interaction should ideally produce visible feedback.

A kid should feel:

> I clicked something and my game changed.

Not:

> I completed another configuration step.

---

# Visual Brand

The Splat Lab! visual style is:

- bright
- colorful
- comic
- toy-like
- energetic
- friendly
- polished
- slightly chaotic

The brand should feel closer to a cartoon game box, comic workshop, or animated science lab than a SaaS dashboard.

---

## Logo Style

The established Splat Lab! logo uses:

- chunky cartoon lettering
- bright yellow/orange letter faces
- red dimensional shading
- dark outlines
- glossy highlights
- exaggerated perspective
- playful irregularity

The logo should feel energetic and physical rather than flat and corporate.

Cooper may appear with the primary logo, especially in hero branding.

A text-only compact version can use:

**Splat**  
**Lab!**

in the same visual style.

---

## Color Direction

The current visual language strongly favors:

- bright sky blue
- yellow
- orange
- red
- white
- dark navy/black outlines
- green game-world accents

The exact palette can evolve, but the overall feeling should remain bright, high-contrast, and playful.

Avoid muted corporate palettes.

---

# UI Branding

## Builder Experience

The primary builder layout uses two major areas:

### Left side

Conversation and guided choices with Cooper.

Example:

> **Cooper:** What do you want to make?

- Platformer
- Top-down adventure
- Endless runner
- Maze

Then:

> **Cooper:** What theme do you want?

- Dragons
- Pirates
- Ninjas
- Space

Then:

> **Cooper:** Pick your hero!

Visual sprite choices appear.

### Right side

A live playable game preview.

The game should update as choices are made.

The child should not need to wait until the end to generate the game.

Example:

Platformer selected  
-> playable platformer appears

Dragons selected  
-> world becomes dragon-themed

Wizard selected  
-> wizard becomes the hero

Cannon added  
-> Cooper may appear to test the cannon

---

# Interaction Principles

## Buttons First, AI Second

When a child presses a predefined button, the app should generally update game state directly.

Example:

**[Platformer]**

should deterministically set:

```json
{
  "engine": "platformer"
}
```

An AI call is unnecessary.

AI is most useful for freeform requests such as:

> Make the wizard shoot exploding chickens.

The AI should translate the request into safe, supported game capabilities.

---

## Every Click Should Do Something Fun

Whenever possible:

- immediately update the preview
- animate the change
- let Cooper react
- demonstrate the feature

Do not make the kid answer a long sequence of questions before seeing anything happen.

---

# Cooper in Loading & Progress States

Avoid generic loading indicators when Cooper can communicate progress.

Instead of:

> Generating...

Use concepts such as:

> Cooper is testing it...

> Cooper is wiring things up...

> Cooper is checking the gravity...

> Cooper is looking for more slime...

Short animations can show him:

- running with tools
- hammering something
- flying past the screen
- being launched
- emerging from smoke
- checking a clipboard
- giving a thumbs-up after an explosion

---

# Cooper in Empty States

Cooper is useful for making otherwise boring UI states feel intentional.

### No games yet

> Nothing here yet. Let's make something.

### Failed build

Cooper crawls out of smoke.

> Okay. That did not work.

### Game saved

Cooper stamps a clipboard.

> Saved!

### Network error

Cooper holds an unplugged cable.

> I found the problem. Probably.

---

# Multiplayer

Splat Lab! supports playing games online with **1-4 friends**.

For the early product, multiplayer should feel lightweight and safe.

Prefer:

- invite links
- room codes
- generated nicknames
- predefined emotes

Avoid making Splat Lab! feel like a social network.

Cooper can guide multiplayer setup:

> Want some backup?

> Share this code with your friends.

---

# Safety Tone

Splat Lab! is intended for kids, so Cooper's slapstick should remain cartoon-safe.

Allowed:

- flattened like a pancake
- covered in soot
- dizzy eyes
- singed feathers
- slime
- bouncing
- flying through walls
- getting launched
- cartoon explosions
- bandages

Avoid:

- blood
- gore
- broken bones
- realistic injury
- prolonged suffering
- cruelty
- fear that feels genuinely traumatic

Think classic cartoon resilience.

---

When creating new Cooper artwork, verify:

- [ ] White feathers.
- [ ] Red comb and wattle.
- [ ] Orange/yellow beak and feet.
- [ ] Large expressive eyes.
- [ ] Goggles are present.
- [ ] **Exactly two tan bandages cross in an X on the top of his beak.**
- [ ] **One small red X-shaped scratch appears on each cheek when both cheeks are visible.**
- [ ] No cheek bandage is used as part of his standard design.
- [ ] No extra permanent facial scratches or grime are added.
- [ ] His face is otherwise clean.
- [ ] Feather rendering is relatively simple and smooth, not overly detailed.
- [ ] Lab coat is present when appropriate.
- [ ] Lab coat is mostly clean, with only faint scuffs or smudges by default.
- [ ] The **Cooper** badge appears only where it would physically be visible; omit it from rear views.
- [ ] Cooper looks slightly rumpled or battle-tested without looking dirty or seriously injured.
- [ ] Temporary slime, soot, dust, or other slapstick effects are clearly scene-specific.
- [ ] Expression is clear and readable.
- [ ] Art feels colorful and kid-friendly.
- [ ] Cooper looks resilient, not genuinely injured.
- [ ] The humor comes from experimentation and chaos.
- [ ] Cooper remains visually consistent with the Splat Lab! logo and UI.

---

# Brand Consistency Checklist

When creating Splat Lab! UI or marketing assets:

- [ ] Use the name **Splat Lab!**
- [ ] Keep the exclamation point.
- [ ] Use a bright, comic, game-like visual style.
- [ ] Favor chunky shapes and strong outlines.
- [ ] Keep Cooper prominent when a mascot is useful.
- [ ] Make creation feel like experimentation.
- [ ] Avoid corporate/SaaS visual language.
- [ ] Keep language simple and playful.
- [ ] Emphasize making and playing games rather than "using AI."
- [ ] Let kids feel ownership of what they create.
- [ ] Prefer immediate visual feedback.
- [ ] Keep the experience fun even when something fails.

**Cooper is Splat Lab!'s cheerful, battle-tested chicken lab assistant—recognizable by his oversized goggles, crossed beak bandages, and two cheek scratches—who helps kids build games and somehow ends up personally testing every ridiculous idea they invent.**


# Other Characters

## Cooper

He works at the lab. He is also, somehow, the lab's guinea pig.

He helps kids build games, then personally tests every ridiculous idea they invent.

Cheerful, battle-tested, and first in line when the cannon needs a volunteer.

“Should we test it?”

## Rupert

If something on the hill is broken, dark, sparking, or making a noise it definitely shouldn't be making, somebody has probably already called him.

He brings tools, spare cables, bad jokes, and usually the fix.

“I’ll be there in ten.”

## Jamie

Builder by day. Trader by night.

He measures twice, checks the chart three times, and somehow already knows how this is going to play out.

While the rest of the crew improvises, Jamie has a plan.

“Frames houses by day. Frames trades by night.”

## Vix

You didn't see her come in.

You probably won't see her leave.

She likes shortcuts, locked doors, bad ideas, and knowing where the exit is before anyone else realizes they need one.

Trouble with a plan.

“Wasn’t here.”

## Lango

No fancy rig. No degree. No excuses.

Give him a beat-up laptop, an idea, and a problem everyone else called impossible, and he'll disappear for the night.

By morning, something works that didn't before.

Build. Break. Fix. Repeat. Full send.

## Leenie

She likes cute outfits, perfect hair, being noticed, and pretending she doesn't care what anyone thinks.

She can be a little dramatic. Maybe a little snobby.

But when somebody really needs her, the attitude disappears.

Pretty on the outside. Big heart underneath.
